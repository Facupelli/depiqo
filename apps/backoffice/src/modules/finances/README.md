# Finances

## Product intent

Rental businesses need to understand how much a month's rentals represent, which equipment owners participate in that amount, and what remains for the business. Monthly understanding is the primary goal; annual analysis provides context rather than replacing it.

## Why a separate business area

Understanding commercial performance is a different responsibility from operating rentals, managing equipment ownership, or configuring prices. Finances owns that analytical experience, not the underlying operational workflows.

Financial visibility must not be treated as a consequence of operational responsibility: staff may need to operate rentals without seeing aggregate financial information, while someone reviewing finances may not operate rentals at all.

## Business boundary

This area currently supports analysis of agreed commercial commitments, not bookkeeping. Rental agreements alone cannot establish whether money was collected, owners were paid, or the business made a profit. Any future payment, settlement, or profitability capability needs authoritative facts for those questions rather than a reinterpretation of agreed rental amounts.
